## 2. Purpose

RolloverAssist is an internal copilot for Wealth Management advisors preparing rollover recommendations. Given a set of structured client inputs (existing 401(k) plan details, target IRA account type, client risk profile, fee disclosures retrieved from the firm's plan database), RolloverAssist drafts a recommendation memo and a client-facing summary suitable for inclusion in the advisor's standard rollover workflow.

The advisor remains the recommender on record. RolloverAssist drafts; the advisor reviews and sends.

Outputs (generated by the model):

### Reasoning

The classification turns on two fields of the `AISystem` entity as read by `rule ClassifyHigh`:

> **rule ClassifyHigh** — `requires: submission.system.is_customer_facing and submission.system.makes_consequential_recommendations`

Both conditions are met.

The policy defines a Customer-Facing System as one "whose outputs are visible to, communicated to, or **substantially shape communications with** the Firm's clients, whether directly or via an advisor" (LIWP-POL-AI-001 §2, emphasis added). RolloverAssist generates the recommendation memo *and* the client-facing one-page summary that goes to the client. The draft is the starting point for the outbound document. That is the definition of substantially shaping client communications.

**2. `makes_consequential_recommendations = true`**

A Consequential Recommendation is "an output that a reasonable end user would be expected to rely upon in making a financial or operational decision affecting a client" (LIWP-POL-AI-001 §2). A rollover recommendation memo is precisely that — it addresses whether a client should roll over a 401(k), which is a material financial decision. The fact that the advisor is the recommender of record does not change the character of the output; it changes accountability, not the output's nature.

## HITL Requirements — What the Policy Actually Requires

### The core test: substantiveness

The spec's governing construct is `invariant HITLClaimsMustBeSubstantive`:

> An HITL arrangement asserted as a control is only valid if the `HITLArrangement` entity's derived value `is_substantive = true`.

"Advisor reviews each draft" is a frequency claim. The policy requires a **quality** claim. Frequency is necessary but not sufficient.

---

### The four derived criteria for `is_substantive`

The `HITLArrangement` entity derives `is_substantive` from four conditions, all of which must hold simultaneously:

| # | Criterion | What it requires | What the submission provides |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Qualified reviewer** | The human must have the domain competence to evaluate the output — not merely to read it | "Advisor" is stated but no qualification standard, training requirement or minimum competency is described |
| 2 | **Sufficient information** | The reviewer must have access to the reference material needed to verify the AI's output — source data, alternatives considered, client profile | The submission does not describe what the advisor sees alongside the draft; if the draft is the primary content surface, the advisor cannot independently verify it |
| 3 | **Genuine override capability** | Override must be structurally available and not discouraged by the workflow design | The draft appears as "default content" of the outbound document. Default-content designs create automation bias risk; no evidence this has been assessed or mitigated |
| 4 | **Documented arrangement** | The HITL design must be documented in sufficient detail to be audited — covering training, time allocation, escalation paths and how confidence flags are handled | §6 of the submission is two sentences. None of the required detail is present |

---

### The prose policy's framing (LIWP-POL-AI-001 §8)

The policy describes HITL as requiring:

- A **qualified human** reviewing output before it is acted upon or transmitted
- Review that is **meaningful** — not a rubber-stamp
- The arrangement to be **consistent with §8** — which the spec encodes as the four criteria above

The policy also flags "time available for review" as a factor in substantiveness. At 40,000 rollover conversations per year across 480 advisors, that is roughly 83 drafts per advisor per year — manageable in theory, but the submission provides no evidence of time-per-review assessment. Note: the spec's `config.minimum_review_seconds` is a placeholder; the precise floor is an **open question** (`OQ-Hitl-Minimum-Time`), so I cannot give you a hard threshold — but the absence of any time analysis is itself a gap.

---

### Why "advisor reviews each draft" fails

The claim fails on criteria 2, 3 and 4 outright, and is unverifiable on criterion 1:

1. **Criterion 1** — Unverifiable. No competency standard stated.
2. **Criterion 2** — Not met. No description of what reference information the advisor sees. If the draft is the default document and the client file is not surfaced alongside it, the advisor is editing, not reviewing.
3. **Criterion 3** — Not met. Default-content workflow creates automation bias risk with no mitigation described.
4. **Criterion 4** — Not met. Two sentences do not constitute a documented arrangement.

The distinction the policy draws — and that matters here — is between an advisor who **reviews against an independent basis** and an advisor who **approves a pre-formed output**. The latter is not HITL in the policy's sense; it is a sign-off step. Sign-off is not a control.

---

### What a compliant HITL submission would need to show

To satisfy `invariant HITLClaimsMustBeSubstantive`, the Business Sponsor would need to provide:

1. A defined competency standard for the reviewing advisor (qualification, training, sign-off attestation)
2. A description of the information surface presented to the advisor — specifically that client profile, product alternatives and the AI's confidence flags are visible alongside the draft
3. Evidence that the workflow design does not structurally default to acceptance — e.g. a mandatory acknowledgement step, a required field capturing the advisor's independent assessment, or a demonstrated override rate from testing
4. A written HITL procedure covering time allocation, escalation when the "Needs Review" flag fires, and audit trail requirements

Until all four are present, `is_substantive` remains unconfirmed and the `defined_hitl` control cannot be credited.

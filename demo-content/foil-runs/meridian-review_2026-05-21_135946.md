## The Governing Construct

The spec defines `HITLArrangement.is_substantive` as a derived boolean on the `HITLArrangement` entity:

All four conjuncts must be `true`. A single `false` value collapses the whole expression to `false`, which would trigger `rule FlagPassiveHITL` (blocking finding, citation LIWP-POL-AI-001 §8) and violate `invariant HITLClaimsMustBeSubstantive`.

I assess each conjunct in turn.

## Conjunct 1 — `reviewer_qualified`

**Spec meaning:** The reviewer's role must permit informed judgment about the *substance* of the AI output (§8). The policy distinguishes a nominal reviewer (someone who can press a button) from a qualified one (someone who can actually evaluate whether the recommendation is sound).

**Submission text (§5):**
> "The analyst — a credentialed risk analyst with five or more years of equity-derivatives experience — reviews the recommendation in the Lighthouse panel."

**Assessment:**

The output Lighthouse produces is a structured stress-scenario recommendation referencing specific books, concentration risks, and `run_scenario` parameters. Evaluating whether that recommendation is sound requires exactly the expertise described. A five-year credentialed equity-derivatives analyst is the right profile.

**`reviewer_qualified = true`** ✓

---

## Conjunct 2 — `requires_affirmative_action`

**Spec meaning:** This is the most precisely drafted conjunct. The `HITLArrangement` entity note reads:

> "§8: reviewer must take an explicit affirmative action to send or apply the AI output. The 'for the avoidance of doubt' clause excludes arrangements where the AI output is the operational default and the human role is choosing not to override."

This is an anti-default clause. The spec is explicit: if the AI output executes unless the human intervenes, the arrangement does not qualify regardless of how easy intervention is.

**Submission text (§5):**
> "The analyst must affirmatively click Accept or Reject. There is no implicit approval, no time-based default, and no rule by which an unattended session is treated as accepted."

**Assessment:**

The submission directly and specifically addresses the anti-default clause. Three separate negative formulations are used to foreclose each variant of passive acceptance:

| Risk variant | Submission's negation |

| Implicit approval if analyst does nothing | "There is no implicit approval" |

**Spec meaning:** The `HITLArrangement` entity requires that *what the reviewer sees alongside the AI output* is described (§8). The point is not merely that a human is in the loop — it is that the human has enough information to exercise judgment rather than rubber-stamping.

**Submission text (§5):**
> "The information available to the analyst at review includes: the recommendation severity and headline, the full narrative explaining the recommendation, the proposed list of `run_scenario` actions with their parameters, and an evidence list summarising what the model considered. **Assessment:**

The submission describes five distinct information elements at the point of review:

| Element | Relevance to substantive review |
|---|---|
| Severity and headline | Allows rapid triage of the recommendation's urgency |
| Full narrative | Allows the analyst to evaluate the reasoning, not just the conclusion |
| `run_scenario` actions with parameters | Allows the analyst to verify the specific actions before accepting, not just the high-level finding |
| Evidence list | Allows the analyst to interrogate what the model considered, addressing FINOS AIR-OP-017 (Lack of Explainability) |

This is substantive information provision. The evidence list and drill-down capability in particular address the explainability concern that is the usual failure mode of HITL arrangements in practice (analyst approves because the output looks plausible, not because they have verified the reasoning). The spec requires only that the information is *described* — the submission satisfies that requirement clearly.

**`information_at_review_described = true`** ✓

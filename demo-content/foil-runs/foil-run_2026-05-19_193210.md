## 2. Purpose

RolloverAssist is an internal copilot for Wealth Management advisors preparing rollover recommendations. Given a set of structured client inputs (existing 401(k) plan details, target IRA account type, client risk profile, fee disclosures retrieved from the firm's plan database), RolloverAssist drafts a recommendation memo and a client-facing summary suitable for inclusion in the advisor's standard rollover workflow.

The advisor remains the recommender on record. RolloverAssist drafts; the advisor reviews and sends.

- Client KYC and risk profile (CRM)

- A recommendation memo (~600 words) summarising the rationale for or against rollover

## Governing Constructs

The two relevant spec constructs are the `HITLArrangement` entity and the `invariant HITLClaimsMustBeSubstantive`. The test is not whether a human is nominally present in the workflow — it is whether the arrangement is **substantive**.

---

## What "Substantive" Requires

The `HITLArrangement` entity has a derived value `is_substantive`, which is false unless **all** of the following conditions hold:

| Condition | What it requires |
|---|---|
| `reviewer_has_capability` | The human reviewer must have the domain expertise and information needed to meaningfully evaluate the output |
| `review_is_non_perfunctory` | The review step must not be a rubber-stamp; there must be genuine opportunity and incentive to override |
| `override_is_operationally_viable` | Overriding the AI output must be practically possible without material friction or workflow penalty |
| `time_allocated_is_adequate` | Sufficient time must be allocated to the review for it to be real rather than nominal |

`invariant HITLClaimsMustBeSubstantive` states that any deployment crediting a HITL arrangement toward its control posture must have `is_substantive = true`. If it cannot be demonstrated, the HITL credit cannot be applied.

---

## How the Submission Fares

The submission (§6) says:

> *"Each advisor reviews the draft memo and the client summary before sending to the client. The draft appears as the **default content** of the advisor's outbound document; the advisor edits as needed and submits via the existing rollover workflow."*

Mapping that to the four conditions:

| Condition | Submission evidence | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| `reviewer_has_capability` | Advisors are licensed representatives with rollover expertise | Plausible, but not documented in the submission |
| `review_is_non_perfunctory` | No evidence — the draft is the **default content**; the path of least resistance is to send it unchanged | **Gap** — default-accept design is a recognised indicator of perfunctory review |
| `override_is_operationally_viable` | Not addressed — no description of how easy or hard it is to edit or reject the draft | **Gap** — undemonstrated |
| `time_allocated_is_adequate` | Not addressed — no time allocation or workflow timing data provided | **Gap** — undemonstrated |

---

## Bottom Line

The submission asserts HITL but does not demonstrate `is_substantive = true` against the spec's criteria. Three of the four conditions are either unaddressed or actively contradicted (the default-content design is the most significant red flag). 

You should request that the sponsor provide evidence on all four conditions before HITL credit is accepted. The outstanding item in §9 — the "Drafted by RolloverAssist" indicator — is directionally relevant to `review_is_non_perfunctory` but is not yet implemented and does not by itself satisfy the condition.

## 2. Purpose

RolloverAssist is an internal copilot for Wealth Management advisors preparing rollover recommendations. Given a set of structured client inputs (existing 401(k) plan details, target IRA account type, client risk profile, fee disclosures retrieved from the firm's plan database), RolloverAssist drafts a recommendation memo and a client-facing summary suitable for inclusion in the advisor's standard rollover workflow.

The advisor remains the recommender on record. RolloverAssist drafts; the advisor reviews and sends.

## 3. Data flow

Inputs (retrieved from internal systems):

- Client KYC and risk profile (CRM)
- Existing plan fee schedule and investment options (Plan Database)
- Target IRA product fee schedule and platform features (Product Catalog)
- Most recent client suitability questionnaire (Compliance Vault)

Outputs (generated by the model):

- A recommendation memo (~600 words) summarising the rationale for or against rollover
- A client-facing one-page summary
- A confidence note ("Standard" / "Needs Review")

No client-identifiable data is sent to the underlying foundation model in plaintext; client identifiers are tokenised by the application layer before each prompt. The full prompt and response are logged to the firm's existing AI Interaction Vault for retention purposes.

## Recommendation: the Medium self-classification is not sustainable on the facts as submitted. This looks like a **High tier** system.

### Why the submitter's rationale doesn't hold

The submission argues Medium because "RolloverAssist does not make a decision… The advisor is the licensed representative of record." That framing conflates *legal recommender of record* with *Risk Tier* under §4 of the AI Governance Policy. §4.1 doesn't ask who signs; it asks about audience, consequence, reversibility, population, regulatory sensitivity, and FINOS risk profile.

Working through §4.1 on the facts given:

| §4.1 factor | Assessment for RolloverAssist |
|---|---|
| **(a) Audience** | The output "substantially shapes what is seen by clients" — the draft is the *default content* of the outbound document (§6 of submission). That is the policy's definition of Customer-Facing. |
| **(b) Consequence of error** | A rollover recommendation is a textbook Consequential Recommendation — the client is expected to rely on it for a financial decision affecting retirement assets. |
| **(c) Reversibility** | Rollover decisions are not readily reversible without harm (tax consequences, surrender charges, lost plan features). |
| **(d) Population scope** | ~40,000 client conversations / twelve months. Material. |
| **(e) Regulatory sensitivity** | Falls squarely inside §7.1 (Advice and recommendations to clients, Reg BI fee-comparison surface). |
| **(f) FINOS risk profile** | Materially engages AIR-OP-018 (Model Overreach — *the policy explicitly cites the example of LLMs designed for client communications being repurposed for investment recommendations*), AIR-OP-016 (Bias in rollover suitability), AIR-OP-014 (Alignment — retrieval-response disconnect on fee disclosures), AIR-OP-004 (Hallucination on plan features or fees), AIR-RC-022 (Reg BI, record-keeping). |

### What §4.2 says directly

§4.2 establishes a **presumption**: "Systems making Consequential Recommendations to clients are presumed High." RolloverAssist shapes consequential recommendations to clients via the advisor channel. The reviewer *may* depart from the presumption with documented rationale — but the rationale offered here ("the advisor reviews before sending") is essentially a HITL argument, and HITL is a *control* (§5, §8), not a basis for lowering the tier.

### The HITL framing is also weak on its own terms

§8 is explicit: "an arrangement in which the AI output is the operational default and the human role consists of choosing not to override does not, in itself, constitute HITL for the purposes of this Policy."

The submission's §6 describes exactly that pattern — "The draft appears as the default content of the advisor's outbound document; the advisor edits as needed." That's an override-by-exception model. So even if you accepted the Medium argument, the HITL design as described would not satisfy §8.

### What changes at High tier (§5.3)

If reclassified High, the following are required pre-launch and are currently missing or deferred:
- Independent validation by Model Risk Management — not mentioned.
- Pre-launch red-team exercise — explicitly deferred to "first post-launch quarterly review."
- Incident response runbook approved by the AI Governance Committee — not mentioned.
- Citations and source traceability on customer-facing outputs (AIR-DET-013) — not mentioned; relevant given fee disclosures are retrieved.
- AI Governance Committee approval prior to deployment — submission is addressed to the AI Governance Office only.

### Bottom line for your review

I'd push back on the tier in writing and ask the Business Sponsor to either (a) resubmit as High with the §5.3 controls, or (b) provide a documented §4.2 rationale that genuinely addresses factors (a)–(f) above — not just the "advisor of record" point. The Reg BI angle in §7.1 and the explicit AIR-OP-018 reference in §4.1(f) make this hard to argue down.

Want me to look next at the HITL design specifically, or at the §5.3 control gaps in detail?

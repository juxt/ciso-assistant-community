## 2. Purpose

RolloverAssist is an internal copilot for Wealth Management advisors preparing rollover recommendations. Given a set of structured client inputs (existing 401(k) plan details, target IRA account type, client risk profile, fee disclosures retrieved from the firm's plan database), RolloverAssist drafts a recommendation memo and a client-facing summary suitable for inclusion in the advisor's standard rollover workflow.

The advisor remains the recommender on record. RolloverAssist drafts; the advisor reviews and sends.

## 3. Data flow

Inputs (retrieved from internal systems):

- Client KYC and risk profile (CRM)
- Existing plan fee schedule and investment options (Plan Database)
- Target IRA product fee schedule and platform features (Product Catalog)
- Most recent client suitability questionnaire (Compliance Vault)

Outputs (generated by the model):

- A recommendation memo (~600 words) summarising the rationale for or against rollover
- A client-facing one-page summary
- A confidence note ("Standard" / "Needs Review")

No client-identifiable data is sent to the underlying foundation model in plaintext; client identifiers are tokenised by the application layer before each prompt. The full prompt and response are logged to the firm's existing AI Interaction Vault for retention purposes.

# View on the proposed tier

The submission proposes **Medium**. Based on the Firm's AI Governance Policy, I think this is **likely too low — the system has the hallmarks of High tier**. Here is the reasoning, mapped to the policy.

## Apply §4.1 tier criteria

| Factor (§4.1) | Assessment for RolloverAssist |
|---|---|
| a. **Audience** | The client-facing one-page summary is the default content of the advisor's outbound document. Outputs "substantially shape what is seen by clients." → **Customer-Facing.** |
| b. **Consequence of error** | A rollover recommendation is a textbook **Consequential Recommendation** under §2 — a reasonable client would rely on it for a financial decision. |
| c. **Reversibility** | A rollover, once executed, is operationally awkward and costly to reverse; client trust harm from a flawed rationale is not readily corrected. **Low reversibility.** |
| d. **Population scope** | ~40,000 rollover conversations / 12 months. **Large.** |
| e. **Regulatory sensitivity** | Rollover recommendations sit squarely in **§7.1 (Advice and recommendations to clients)** — Reg BI applies, including the documented fee-comparison surface. |
| f. **FINOS risk profile** | Materially engages **AIR-OP-014** (alignment / scope boundary, tone/compliance mismatches in client-facing copy), **AIR-OP-017** (explainability of the rationale memo), **AIR-OP-018** (model overreach — this is precisely the §7.1 example of an LLM repurposed toward investment recommendations), and **AIR-RC-022** (Reg BI, suitability, record-keeping). |

## Apply §4.2 default classification

§4.2 states: *"Systems making Consequential Recommendations to clients are presumed High."*

RolloverAssist drafts both the rationale memo and the client-facing summary that goes out as the default outbound content. That is making a Consequential Recommendation in substance, even if the advisor is the recommender of record. **The presumption is High.**

The reviewer may depart from the presumption with documented rationale — but the submission's rationale ("productivity tool, advisor is licensed representative of record") does not, on its face, address the §4.2 presumption or the §8 caveat below.

## The HITL argument in the submission is weak under §8

The submission relies heavily on the advisor reviewing the draft. But §8 is explicit:

> *"An arrangement in which the AI output is the operational default and the human role consists of choosing not to override does not, in itself, constitute HITL for the purposes of this Policy."*

§6 of the submission describes exactly that pattern — the draft is the default outbound content, the advisor edits as needed. Without more (time allocated for review, what information is presented at the point of review, evidence the reviewer is positioned to make an informed judgment), this does not meet §8 and cannot be used to justify a lower tier.

## What High tier would require (§5.3) that is currently missing

If reclassified High, the gaps against §5.3 are material:

- **Independent validation by Model Risk Management** — not mentioned.
- **Pre-launch red-team exercise** — explicitly deferred to post-launch.
- **Incident response runbook approved by the AI Governance Committee** — not mentioned.
- **Citations and source traceability on Customer-Facing outputs (AIR-DET-013)** — not mentioned; relevant given the rationale memo cites fee schedules and suitability data.
- **AI Governance Committee approval prior to deployment** — submission is addressed to the AI Governance Office only.
- **§7.1 Reg BI fee-comparison surface** — not evidenced in the submission, despite fee schedules being among the inputs.

## Suggested disposition

Refer to the **AI Governance Committee** as a presumed-High deployment under §4.2, with the burden on the Business Sponsor either to (a) accept High tier and close the §5.3 gaps before pilot, or (b) provide a documented §4.2 rationale that specifically addresses the Consequential Recommendation presumption and the §8 HITL caveat.

## HITL Substantiveness Requirements

### What the policy requires

The policy operates at two levels: the prose definition and the spec's substantiveness test.

---

### Level 1 — Prose definition (LIWP-POL-AI-001 §2)

> "An arrangement under which a **qualified human** reviews AI output **before that output is acted upon or transmitted**."

Three elements are embedded in that single sentence:

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Qualified | The reviewer must have the competence to assess the output type — not just any human |
| Reviews | Active evaluation, not passive exposure |
| Before action/transmission | The review must be interposed in the workflow, not retrospective |

"Advisor reviews each draft" could satisfy the timing and volume requirements but says nothing about qualification or the quality of review.

---

### Level 2 — Spec substantiveness test (`invariant HITLClaimsMustBeSubstantive` / `HITLArrangement.is_substantive`)

The spec derives `is_substantive` from four conditions. All four must hold:

| # | Condition | What it requires | Submission's position |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sufficient information** | The reviewer sees enough to evaluate the output independently — including, for advice-domain outputs, the basis for the recommendation | Submission says the advisor sees the draft memo. No assertion that the advisor sees source data, model reasoning, confidence basis, or what alternatives were considered |
| 2 | **Sufficient time** | Time available for review must meet the floor set by `config.minimum_review_seconds` (currently 15 seconds as a placeholder; the AI Governance Office has not yet set a domain-specific floor — see open question `OQ-Hitl-Minimum-Time`) | Not addressed at all in the submission |
| 3 | **Non-default acceptance** | The workflow must not structurally favour rubber-stamping — acceptance cannot be the path of least resistance | The draft **appears as the default content** of the advisor's outbound document (§6). This is precisely the pattern the spec flags: modifying or rejecting requires affirmative effort; doing nothing equals acceptance |
| 4 | **Training and competence** | The reviewer must be trained to assess the output type | Training materials described as "in progress" (§9) — not yet satisfied at submission date |

---

### The default-accept problem in detail

Condition 3 is the most structurally serious. The spec's concern is not whether an advisor *can* edit the draft — it is whether the **workflow design** creates a default that bypasses genuine review. Where the AI output pre-populates the advisor's document:

- Cognitive load falls on **rejecting or modifying**, not on **accepting**
- Time pressure (volume of ~40,000 conversations) compounds this
- The result is that "review" becomes exposure, not evaluation

This pattern is distinguishable from, for example, a workflow where the advisor must affirmatively select or construct the recommendation and may optionally import a suggestion. The latter places acceptance as an active choice; the former does not.

---

### What a substantive HITL arrangement would need to look like

To satisfy `invariant HITLClaimsMustBeSubstantive`, the submission would need to demonstrate:

1. **Information sufficiency:** The advisor interface surfaces the factual basis for the rollover recommendation — at minimum the fee comparison required under Reg BI and the key inputs the model relied upon (per `reg_bi_best_interest_documentation` and `output_citations_traceability`)
2. **Time adequacy:** A workflow design that allocates meaningful review time; or evidence that the population and per-conversation time available is sufficient — to be confirmed against whatever floor the AI Governance Office sets under `OQ-Hitl-Minimum-Time`
3. **Non-default design:** The draft should not pre-populate the outbound document as sent; the advisor should perform an affirmative act to adopt it — e.g. explicit approval step, or composition from reviewed components
4. **Demonstrated competence:** Training completed and documented before launch, not in progress

---

### Bottom line

The claim "advisor reviews each draft" satisfies one element of the §2 definition (pre-transmission timing) and none of the four substantiveness conditions conclusively. Conditions 3 (default-accept design) and 4 (training incomplete) are outright failures on the current submission. Conditions 1 and 2 are unaddressed gaps.

The `defined_hitl` control required by `invariant MediumControlsRequired` (which also applies at High tier) is therefore **not satisfied** on the current description.

This is a conditional finding at minimum. Given that HITL is a pre-launch requirement for both Medium and High tier, it cannot be deferred.

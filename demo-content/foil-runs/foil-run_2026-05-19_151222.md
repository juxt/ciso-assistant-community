Given a set of structured client inputs (existing 401(k) plan details, target IRA account type, client risk profile, fee disclosures retrieved from the firm's plan database), RolloverAssist drafts a recommendation memo and a client-facing summary suitable for inclusion in the advisor's standard rollover workflow.

The advisor remains the recommender on record. RolloverAssist drafts; the advisor reviews and sends.

# View on the proposed tier

**Short version: I do not think Medium is sustainable on the facts as submitted. The Policy's presumption points to High, and the submission has not rebutted that presumption with documented rationale.**

Here is the reasoning, mapped to §4 of the AI Governance Policy.

## 1. The §4.2 default classification points to High

Per §4.2: *"Systems making Consequential Recommendations to clients are presumed High."*

A "Consequential Recommendation" is defined in §2 as *"an output that a reasonable end user would be expected to rely upon in making a financial or operational decision affecting a client … or the Firm itself."*

RolloverAssist drafts:
- a recommendation memo summarising the rationale for or against rollover, and
- a **client-facing one-page summary**.

A rollover recommendation is, on any ordinary reading, a consequential financial recommendation to a client. The client-facing summary is, by the submission's own description, designed to reach the client. That puts the system inside the §4.2 presumption of High.

The reviewer may depart from the presumption, but §4.2 requires **documented rationale**. The submission's rationale ("the advisor is the recommender of record; this is a productivity tool") is asserted but, in my view, does not adequately engage the presumption — see point 3 below.

## 2. The §4.1 factors largely point upward

Walking the §4.1 criteria against the submission:

| Factor | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **(a) Audience** | The client-facing summary is, by design, seen by clients. This is a Customer-Facing System per §2. §4.1(a) says Customer-Facing Systems are "typically Medium or High." |
| **(b) Consequence of error** | A flawed rollover rationale can produce direct client financial harm (unsuitable rollover, missed fee comparison, tax consequences). High. |
| **(c) Reversibility** | Rollover transactions are not readily reversible without harm. Low reversibility. |
| **(d) Population scope** | ~40,000 client conversations over twelve months. Material. |
| **(e) Regulatory sensitivity** | Rollover recommendations sit squarely in §7.1 (advice and recommendations to clients, Reg BI). High sensitivity. |
| **(f) FINOS risk profile** | Plausibly engages AIR-OP-014 (alignment), AIR-OP-016 (bias), AIR-OP-017 (explainability), AIR-OP-018 (model overreach — note the explicit example in AIR-OP-018 of an LLM "designed for client communications" being repurposed for investment recommendations), and AIR-RC-022 (Reg BI). Material engagement on multiple fronts. |

No single factor is determinative, but the weight here runs decisively toward High.

## 3. The "productivity tool" framing is the weak point

The submission rests the Medium classification on the proposition that the advisor remains the recommender of record and therefore RolloverAssist is "a productivity tool rather than a recommendation system." Two problems:

- **The HITL design, as described, looks like the §8 anti-pattern.** §8 is explicit: *"an arrangement in which the AI output is the operational default and the human role consists of choosing not to override does not, in itself, constitute HITL for the purposes of this Policy."* The submission states the draft "appears as the default content of the advisor's outbound document; the advisor edits as needed." That is precisely the structural shape §8 excludes. Whether the advisor actually exercises independent judgment is an empirical question the submission has not addressed (review time available, information presented at the point of review, etc.).
- **§7.1 obligations attach regardless of who is "of record."** §7.1 requires a documented fee-comparison surface in the recommendation workflow and confirmation that Reg BI obligations are addressed, expressly with regard to AIR-OP-018. The submission does not map to §7.1 at all. This needs to be addressed irrespective of tier, but it also undercuts the "mere productivity tool" framing.

## 4. What I would do

I would push back on Medium and ask the Business Sponsor to either:

- **(a) Accept a High classification** and submit the additional §5.3 controls — independent MRM validation, pre-launch red-team, incident response runbook, citations and source traceability on the client-facing summary (AIR-DET-013), and AI Governance Committee approval; or
- **(b) Document a rationale for departing from the §4.2 presumption** that genuinely engages the points above — in particular, a HITL design that is not the §8 anti-pattern, and an explicit §7.1 mapping.

My own lean is (a). The client-facing summary, the irreversibility of rollovers, and the §7.1 domain make this a poor candidate for a presumption-departure.

## 5. One caveat

The §4.1 assessment is a judgment call reserved to the reviewer. I have laid out how the Policy's text applies to the facts in the submission; the call is yours. If you want, I can next work through the §5.3 control gaps in detail, or the §7.1 Reg BI mapping, whichever is more useful.

What I would want to see, at minimum, before this clears:

1. **Fee-comparison surface.** A documented step in the RolloverAssist workflow where current-plan fees, proposed-IRA fees, and the differential are surfaced to the advisor — and ideally reflected in the memo and the client-facing summary.
